How Lamina works

Five tabs, left to right, in the order you use them. Everything re-slices as you change it.

The workflow

  1. Model — upload your own (STL, OBJ, 3MF, PLY, OFF, GLB, STEP) or pick an example, then set how big you want the finished piece: type one of the size boxes and the other two follow the model's proportions. turn stands it the right way up.
  2. Technique — how the model is taken apart: stacked layers, interlocked slices, a radial fan, ribs along a curve, folded panels, a plaque on a back plate. Each has its own settings underneath.
  3. Sheet & fit — what you cut and on what: material and its density, thickness, sheet size (your machine's bed), and the cut compensation. PLA or PETG print the parts instead, nested on your printer's bed. Not on the list? Type any thickness or sheet size, or a material's density, and press + to keep it.
  4. Checks — whether it holds together. Every error offers one-click fixes; press one and it re-slices.
  5. Export — the cut files (SVG, DXF, PDF, EPS), a 3D-printable prototype, a fit test, the assembly key.

The settings

  • find a setting — type a word in the box over the tabs and only the settings that match stay, from every tab. Esc clears it.
  • a tab is in sections: click a heading to fold it. A setting that another one switches on is set in under it.
  • ↺ beside a bold name: that setting is not at its default. Click the sign to put the default back.
  • click a setting's name and its explanation opens under it. Hover shows the same text.
  • basic / all under the search box: basic shows the settings a first piece needs and names the others at the foot of each group. A setting you changed always shows. The result is the same either way.
  • vary the spacing opens a graph under the tick: the gap between slices against the position along the model. Start from a shape or press follow the model, drag the points, or select one and type its numbers.

In the 3D view

  • drag = orbit · wheel = zoom · right-drag = pan · click the corner cube = flat view
  • lamp over the view puts a bulb inside the model and shows the shadows it throws; bulb height, left–right and front–back move it, and bulb picks a small LED (sharp pattern) or a frosted bulb (soft).
  • the tools at the left of the view do what a key does: pick one, then click or drag the model. Esc goes back to turning the view.
  • drag a coloured ring on the model = turn the whole model · click a part = select it
  • a selected part gets its own handles, on the part itself: the yellow arrow slides it along its normal, the green ring tilts it and the blue ring rolls it, both about the part's own middle, so it turns where it stands (shift-drag and ctrl-drag still do the same two). Only the handles move it: a drag anywhere else, on the part too, turns the view
  • alt-click = add or remove a connection point, a curve point or a radial axis · shift+alt-click = a curve branch out to that point (a leg, an arm, a tail)

Dowels in a stack

  • aligned — the same points straight through every layer, so each dowel is one rod. Alt-click a slice to add a dowel there, alt-click one to remove it; each is listed under dowels with its x, y, to type exactly — that is how you move one.
  • random — new points for every pair of layers, as far apart as each pair allows. They cannot be moved one by one: alt-click switches to aligned, with a dowel where you clicked.
  • lines — straight dowels you draw: a start and an end in the model's x, y, z (0, 0, 0 is its middle). + line draws one up the middle, alt-click one up through the point you click (again on it to remove it); change its numbers to move it. Every pair of layers it passes through gets a hole. Two lines hold each layer, one lets it turn.
  • A layer that nothing joins is never deleted: the Checks tab says so and what would hold it.

Keyboard

  • Ctrl+Z undo · Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redo — the last 60 changes, including a part you moved by accident (⌘ on a Mac); the ↶ undo button in the header does the same
  • ? this help · Esc close a dialog or deselect

When something does not fit

  • Parts bigger than your sheet: the Checks tab says so and offers the size that fits, split (cut into pieces joined by puzzle tabs), or one sheet (one long strip).
  • A big model in this browser version takes minutes where the local version takes seconds — and never a layer of your model goes anywhere: how to run it on your own computer.
  • Something came out wrong? Report it — a sentence, and the report fills in the rest.

1 · Fit test — cut this first

what is the fit test?

Five small joints from this job, each cut with a different slot offset and marked with it: yours, and one and two steps either side. Cut them from the same sheet on the same machine as the parts and push each one together. The loosest that slides in without forcing and still holds is your slot offset: set it on Sheet & fit, under Slot fit, and the parts below come out with it. A few minutes and a scrap, against a sheet of parts that will not go together.
